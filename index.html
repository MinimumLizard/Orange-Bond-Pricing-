Gross saving a year = issue size × (no-enhancement coupon − full-guarantee coupon). Breakeven fee = gross saving a year ÷ covered amount. Net saving = (gross saving − fee × covered amount) × tenor, undiscounted. Arranger fees and other issue costs are excluded. The default fee is a placeholder assumption, not a quote. With a partial guarantee, the saving and the covered amount both scale with the share in this model, so the breakeven fee is broadly the same.

Rule of thumb: every 0.25 point saved on LKR 1bn is LKR 2.5m a year. On – it is – a year.

Methodology

Conventions

  • Sri Lankan Treasury bond yields are quoted on a semi-annual basis. Each coupon is converted to that basis before it is compared with a government yield.
    • Annual-pay to semi-annual basis: annualToSemi(r) = 2 × (√(1 + r) − 1). The Social Bond's 11.15% and 11.40% (paid annually) become 10.86% and 11.09%.
    • Semi-annual basis to annual-pay: semiToAnnual(r) = (1 + r/2)² − 1. Used for the annual-pay equivalents on the cards.
  • Spreads are in percentage points (“+1.50 pts”). Amounts are in Sri Lankan rupees.
  • Government yields are secondary-market levels from Wealth Trust Securities' daily reports, some as republished by the Daily FT. Where a source gives a range (e.g. 9.36%–9.35%), the midpoint is used. For today's yield, the midpoint of the closing offer and bid is used.
  • Reference dates: the government yield is taken as close as possible to the date the coupon was fixed. For the Social Bond that is its prospectus date, 7 Aug 2025. The Green Bond's prospectus date is not public, so its opening date, 17 Feb 2025, is used.

Steps

  1. Historical spread = coupon on a semi-annual basis − the tenor-matched government yield at pricing. Green 3y: 15.02.28. Social 4y (matures 27 Aug 2029): 15.10.29. Social 5y (matures 27 Aug 2030): 01.07.30.
  2. Unsecured spread = the average of the two Social spreads. The two Social spreads give the low and high ends of the range.
  3. Guaranteed spread = the Green spread.
  4. Guarantee effect = unsecured spread − guaranteed spread. This is an estimate from two issues priced on different dates, at different tenors and in different market conditions.
  5. Orange coupons (semi-annual basis), with g = government yield now:
    • No enhancement = g + unsecured spread + tenor adjustment.
    • Full bank guarantee = g + guaranteed spread.
    • Partial DFI guarantee = g + (unsecured spread + tenor adjustment) − share × guarantee effect. At share 0 this equals no enhancement; at share 1 it equals a full guarantee.
  6. Government yield now: the 15.12.29 Treasury bond, midpoint of its closing quote in the latest Wealth Trust daily report. If the Orange Bond issues around December 2026, a 3-year bond matures around December 2029.
  7. Tenor adjustment (default 0.00): the Social spreads come from 4- and 5-year bonds, while the Orange Bond is 3-year. Credit spreads usually rise with tenor, so a 3-year unsecured spread may be slightly tighter, which calls for a negative adjustment. The two Social tranches differ by only about 0.09 points for one extra year, which suggests the effect is small. The adjustment applies to the unsecured spread only, because the guaranteed spread already comes from a 3-year bond.
  8. Economics: interest a year = issue size × coupon. Gross saving from a guarantee = size × (no-enhancement − full-guarantee coupon). Covered amount = size × coverage factor (default 1.10, i.e. principal plus about one year's interest; the Green Bond's guarantee covered LKR 1.107bn on LKR 1bn). Breakeven fee = gross saving a year ÷ covered amount. Net saving = (gross saving − fee × covered amount) × tenor, undiscounted.

Limitations

  • Two data points. The guarantee effect rests on one guaranteed and one unsecured issue. It is an indication, not a statistical estimate.
  • Different dates, tenors and market conditions. The Green Bond priced in February 2025 and the Social Bond in August 2025, with government yields about 0.7 points apart and a different curve shape. The spreads also include whatever premium or discount each market environment and investor base implied.
  • Ratings differ. The guaranteed Green Bond carried LRA's A- at issue (upgraded to A+ in June 2025), in line with the guarantor Seylan Bank. AFC's own entity rating was BBB- then, and BBB (LRA) by the time of the Social Bond and today. The Social Bond priced after that upgrade, and AFC is still BBB, so the unsecured spread reflects AFC's current rating. The Green Bond's spread mainly reflects Seylan Bank's credit, although investors also had recourse to AFC, then rated a notch lower. An Orange Bond guarantee from a different bank or DFI, with a different rating, could be worth more or less.
  • Issue costs excluded. Arranger and placement fees, listing, trustee and rating fees, and any DFI structuring costs are not modelled.
  • The Orange label's own effect on demand is not modelled. A first-of-its-kind gender bond may draw impact investors or DFIs and tighten pricing, or carry a novelty premium. Neither is assumed.
  • Other factors not modelled: issue size relative to demand (both earlier issues were oversubscribed on the opening day); a preferential allotment to identified investors (15 million of the Social Bond's 20 million bonds); the liquidity of a small listed issue; and moves in government yields between now and pricing.
  • The board approved an unsecured bond. The guarantee structures are alternatives to consider, not current plans.
